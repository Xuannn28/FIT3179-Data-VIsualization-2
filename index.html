When Rain Meets Canopy: Rainfall and Forest Coverage (2003–2021)

Although rainfall remains abundant nationwide, forest coverage appears more dependent on land-use policy than on precipitation itself. Borneo states maintain high rainfall and dense forests, while Peninsular states face stability in rainfall but gradual loss in forested area — showing that deforestation is driven more by human expansion than by climate variability.

Growing Cities, Shrinking Forests: Human Pressure (2001–2023)

Population growth outpaces forest preservation, illustrating how urbanization and agriculture expansion continue to reduce forested land. The slight recovery around 2015 suggests temporary success from reforestation programs, but the broader trend indicates sustained ecological pressure.

What Drives the Loss? Human and Natural Causes (2001–2024)

Agricultural expansion dominates forest loss, far exceeding impacts from logging or wildfires. This underscores how economic reliance on plantation crops continues to outweigh conservation goals in Malaysia’s land-use decisions.

Lost Trees, Rising Carbon: Deforestation and CO₂ Emissions (2001–2024)

Peaks in emissions align with major deforestation years, but recent declines after 2017 indicate policy impact and improved forest management. This decoupling between tree loss and emissions suggests better recovery capacity and reduced biomass loss intensity in recent years.
